## 1.Dataset Description
The Marketing Campaign Dataset provides detailed information about 2,240 customers, including demographic attributes, spending habits, and campaign responses. The dataset contains 29 variables such as year of birth, education, income, marital status, number of children and teenagers at home, and spending on various product categories like wine, meat, and gold. Additionally, it records customer response to past marketing campaigns and the date of customer enrollment (Dt_Customer). This dataset offers a rich foundation for predictive modeling and segmentation tasks relevant to customer relationship management.

## 2. Modeling Objective
The primary objective of this phase was to build regression models that predict total customer spending using demographic and behavioral features. We engineered a new target variable, TotalSpent, by summing expenditures across product categories. Our goal was to identify how different features — such as income, education, and family composition — contribute to customer value. We implemented and evaluated two regression models: a standard Linear Regression and a Ridge Regression, applying robust preprocessing and cross-validation strategies to ensure reliable performance estimates.

## 3. Data Preparation & Cleaning
To prepare the dataset for modeling, we first converted the Dt_Customer column to a datetime format and derived customer tenure from it. We handled missing values in key columns like Income through imputation or exclusion, depending on severity. Categorical variables were encoded using OneHotEncoder, with the handle_unknown='ignore' parameter to prevent errors during cross-validation. A composite TotalSpent variable was created by summing monetary features such as MntWines, MntMeatProducts, and others. These steps ensured a clean and consistent input for model training and evaluation.

## 4. Key Insights from EDA
Exploratory data analysis revealed several meaningful patterns. Younger customers tend to spend more across product categories, while income is positively correlated with total spending, especially on premium items like wine and gold. Education and marital status showed clear links to both income and spending levels, with higher-educated and married customers generally spending more. Customers with children or teenagers at home often spend less, likely due to budget constraints. These insights helped inform feature selection and confirmed the feasibility of predicting customer value using available variables.

## 5.Model Evaluation Results
We evaluated model performance using 5-fold cross-validation, focusing on metrics such as R-squared (R²), Mean Squared Error (MSE), and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE). Both Linear Regression and Ridge Regression achieved a mean R² of approximately 0.76, indicating a strong fit to the data. While Ridge Regression offered slightly more stability, the improvement over standard Linear Regression was marginal. The models performed well across most spending levels but struggled slightly with outliers on both ends — particularly very high and very low spenders, which may require alternative modeling strategies.

## 6. Challenges and Resolutions
One key challenge was handling unseen categorical values during cross-validation, which initially caused OneHotEncoder to fail. This was resolved by configuring the encoder with handle_unknown='ignore', allowing it to gracefully handle categories not present in training folds. Another challenge involved missing values in the Income column, which were addressed through a mix of row removal and imputation based on other available features. These preprocessing adjustments were essential for building robust and generalizable models.

## 7. Takeaways
Overall, the project demonstrated that total customer spending can be effectively predicted using demographic and behavioral data. Linear and Ridge regression models both performed well, offering actionable insights into the influence of income, age, education, and family structure on customer value. Key preprocessing steps — especially encoding, feature engineering, and handling missing data — were critical to success. In future iterations, expanding the modeling approach to include non-linear models or segmentation strategies could further enhance prediction accuracy and business insights.
